Argentina

Argentine law, advised by abogados matriculated in Argentina. This page says what that covers, what it does not, and how the Argentine side of a cross-border matter is actually run.

What we advise on under Argentine law

  • Corporate & M&AFormation, governance, share transfers, reorganisations and acquisitions of Argentine entities — including the corporate record work that a foreign buyer will eventually examine.
  • Regulatory & ComplianceAuthorisations, sectoral regulators, exchange and trade regimes, anti-corruption programmes and the reporting obligations that attach to foreign-owned operations.
  • Energy & InfrastructureConcessions, provincial and environmental approvals, project documentation and the operating agreements between partners in different legal systems.
  • TaxArgentine corporate and transactional tax, and the interaction with the United States position on the same transaction.
  • Technology & DataIntellectual property assignment, data protection and technology contracting — including the IP chain that a US financing will test.
  • Disputes & ArbitrationCommercial disputes before Argentine courts and tribunals, and the local steps required to give effect to a foreign award.

What we do not do here

Stated, rather than left to be discovered

  • We do not advise on United States law from Argentina. US questions arising in an Argentine matter are handled by the US entity, and we tell you when a question has crossed.
  • We are not a general Argentine practice. Criminal, family, consumer and administrative work outside the regulatory scope above is not what we do. We will refer rather than stretch.
  • We do not take purely domestic Argentine matters with no cross-border dimension. Excellent firms in Buenos Aires do that work, and they do it better than a corridor practice would.
  • Strategic advisory is a separate, non-legal service provided by a different entity and not covered by professional privilege. See advisory.

How the Argentine side is run

Most of what goes wrong on the Argentine side of a cross-border matter is not a question of Argentine law being unclear. It is a question of Argentine facts being assessed against a standard that will be applied somewhere else, months later, by someone who was not in the room.

So the Argentine work is scoped from the far end. Before diligence begins we establish what the counterparty's counsel will need to see, in the form they will need to see it — and then we go and find out whether it exists. That is a different exercise from advising on Argentine law correctly, and it is the one that determines whether a transaction closes on schedule.
