Entering the United States

For Argentine companies and founders raising US capital, redomiciling, or establishing US operations. Almost everything that stops a round is two years old and was inexpensive to fix at the time.

Who this is for

Argentine companies at the point of raising institutional capital in the United States, founders preparing for that moment, and established businesses opening a US operation whether or not financing is involved.

The common position: a real business with real revenue, an Argentine corporate history assembled while everyone was busy building, and a US investor about to read it against a standard nobody was applying at the time.

The sequence

Seven decisions, in the order they arrive. Each carries the failure we see most often at that point.

Decide what you are actually establishing

A sales presence, a financing vehicle and a full holding structure are three different projects with different costs and different reversibility. The right answer depends on whether you are raising now, later, or not at all.

Where this goes wrong

Founders do the flip because they were told to, before they need to — paying the cost and taking on the compliance burden years ahead of the financing that would have justified it.

Answered by Market Entry Cross-Border Structuring

Audit the intellectual property before you need to

Establish who created what, under which contractual arrangement, and whether assignment was documented at the time rather than reconstructed afterwards. This is the highest-return preparatory work available to you.

Where this goes wrong

Everyone assumes the company owns what it runs on, because it paid for it. Contractors, advisers, university collaborators and pre-incorporation work each break that assumption quietly, and the break surfaces in a data room.

Answered by Technology & Data Corporate & M&A

Reconstruct the Argentine corporate record

Meetings, filings, registrations and the paper trail behind every share issuance since incorporation — assembled while the people who were there can still explain what happened.

Where this goes wrong

Gaps a local adviser would call ordinary and curable read to US counsel as unresolved title to the very shares being exchanged. Curable in weeks at seed. A repricing conversation at Series C.

Answered by Corporate & M&A Argentine practice

Build the structure

Entity formation, the share exchange, and the group architecture that will sit above the Argentine operating company for the rest of its life.

Where this goes wrong

The structure is treated as a translation exercise. It is not: it is a transaction with consequences for every shareholder exchanging an interest, in both countries.

Answered by Cross-Border Structuring Corporate & M&A

Model tax on both sides, before the exchange

The Argentine and United States position on the exchange itself, on the resulting group, and on the eventual exit — assessed jointly.

Where this goes wrong

The structure is optimised for the United States because that is where the investors are, and the Argentine consequence is discovered by the founders personally, after signing.

Answered by Tax Cross-Border Structuring

Understand what the financing documents commit you to

US securities requirements and the representations you will give — about title, about intellectual property, about compliance — every one of which concerns facts sitting in Buenos Aires.

Where this goes wrong

Representations are read as formalities. They are the mechanism by which an unresolved Argentine question becomes a US contractual exposure, and they do not soften because the facts are in another language.

Answered by Private Capital Capital Markets & Finance

Decide the intercompany and equity arrangements

How the Argentine entity is funded, how services between entities are priced, and how equity granted at the top is treated for people employed at the bottom.

Where this goes wrong

None of it is decided. It accumulates as practice, and becomes a transfer pricing, permanent establishment and employee tax question at the worst possible moment.

Answered by Tax Regulatory & Compliance

Five questions to answer before you call anyone

Including us. Every one of these will be asked in diligence. Answering them now costs a week; answering them under a signed term sheet costs considerably more.

  1. Can you prove the company owns the intellectual property the business actually runs on?
  2. Could you produce the paper trail for every share issuance since incorporation?
  3. Do you know what the Argentine entity will still commit you to after the structure is built?
  4. Has anyone modelled the tax consequence of the exchange itself, in both countries, for the founders personally?
  5. Is the intercompany arrangement a documented decision, or a habit?

If you cannot answer three of these, that is not unusual and it is not too late. It is simply the work, and it is cheaper today than it will be.
