Investing in Argentina

For funds, strategics and corporates deploying capital into Argentine energy, mining and infrastructure. The sequence below is the one your investment committee will eventually run. Running it earlier is cheaper.

Who this is for

Private equity and infrastructure funds, strategic acquirers and corporates making a first or renewed allocation to Argentina. Usually with a US or European limited partner base, an investment committee that has not priced Argentine regulatory risk recently, and a timetable set by something other than the diligence.

If your thesis depends on the current investment framework holding, you are in the right place — because that is a legal question rather than an economic one, and it is answerable.

The sequence

Seven decisions, in the order they arrive. Each carries the failure we see most often at that point.

Assess the regime before assessing the asset

Whether the investment is eligible for the incentive framework, what admission requires, and what obligations attach on the far side of approval. This determines the shape of everything downstream, including the vehicle.

Where this goes wrong

Teams read the headline benefits carefully and the attaching commitments quickly. The commitments are the part that binds for thirty years.

Answered by Regulatory & Compliance Risk & Political Economy

Choose the vehicle and the route in

Holding jurisdiction, entity type, and whether this is an acquisition, a joint venture or a greenfield build. Each closes off options that the others keep open.

Where this goes wrong

The structure is chosen for entry and never tested against the exit or the repatriation path. Working backwards from how you leave is cheaper than rebuilding towards it.

Answered by Cross-Border Structuring Tax

Scope diligence from the committee's end

Establish what your investment committee will need to see, in the form it will need to see it — then go and find out whether it exists. That is a different exercise from advising correctly on Argentine law.

Where this goes wrong

The local report is thorough, accurate and unhelpful: it answers Argentine questions in Argentine terms and never addresses what the committee needs to approve. Both sides then wait for the other to translate.

Answered by Corporate & M&A Regulatory & Compliance

Establish whether proceeds can leave

The exchange position as it applies to this investment, this sector and this structure — including what the regime permits today and what it has permitted historically.

Where this goes wrong

Repatriation is modelled as a static assumption rather than a regime that moves. The model then produces a return that depends on a condition nobody is monitoring.

Answered by Regulatory & Compliance Tax

Model tax on both sides, in one exercise

Argentine and United States treatment of the structure, the funding, the distributions and the eventual exit — assessed together rather than sequentially.

Where this goes wrong

Two competent opinions that do not refer to each other. Each is right about its own jurisdiction and neither owns the interaction, which is where the cost sits.

Answered by Tax Cross-Border Structuring

Document under New York law, for Argentine reality

The financing and the transaction documents will be drafted under New York law. The assets, the permits and the counterparties are Argentine, and the drafting has to account for that.

Where this goes wrong

Remedies, notice provisions and security arrangements that are elegant on the page and unavailable in practice, because they were never tested against the procedure that would have to give effect to them.

Answered by Capital Markets & Finance Energy & Infrastructure

Decide protection and enforcement deliberately

Governing law, seat, and whether investment treaty protection is available — chosen at the outset rather than inherited from a precedent document.

Where this goes wrong

A clause carried over from a template produces a favourable award that is not collectable against anything that matters. Winning and recovering are different projects.

Five questions to answer before you call anyone

Including us. If you can answer these, you are further along than most investors who have already signed a term sheet — and you will get more out of any adviser you eventually retain.

  1. Can you state, in one sentence, what would have to change for this thesis to fail?
  2. Do you know whether the target's corporate record actually supports the shares you would be buying?
  3. Has anyone modelled the repatriation path, or only the entry?
  4. Does your Argentine adviser know what your investment committee needs in order to approve this?
  5. Is your governing law a choice, or something you inherited from a precedent document?

If the answer to any of these is uncomfortable, that is the conversation worth having — not a pitch.
