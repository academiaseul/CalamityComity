United States

New York and US federal law, advised by lawyers admitted in New York. This page says what that covers, what it does not, and why so much Argentine business ends up here.

What we advise on under US law

  • Capital Markets & FinanceNew York-law financing and issuance for Argentine issuers and borrowers — the documentation most Argentine cross-border paper is actually written on.
  • Cross-Border StructuringDelaware and US entity formation, holding structures, share exchanges and the group architecture that sits above an Argentine operating company.
  • Private CapitalVenture, growth and private equity investment on US documents — term sheets, financings and the diligence that precedes them.
  • Corporate & M&AAcquisitions and joint ventures where a US buyer or seller sits on one side and an Argentine asset on the other.
  • Disputes & ArbitrationCommercial disputes and international arbitration, including recognition and enforcement of awards against assets that matter.
  • Regulatory & ComplianceSanctions, anti-corruption and trade controls as they apply to a group operating on both sides of the corridor.

Why so much of this sits in New York

Argentine businesses do not choose New York law because they prefer it. They choose it because it is the price of access to institutional capital — a body of contract law with deep precedent, courts that have decided the hard questions, and remedies an international lender can price with confidence.

The practical consequence for an Argentine client is that the most consequential document in their business is often one drafted in a legal tradition they do not operate in, in a language that is not their working language, containing representations about facts that sit in Buenos Aires. Understanding what those representations actually commit them to is not a translation problem. It is the whole problem.

What we do not do here

Stated, rather than left to be discovered

  • We do not advise on Argentine law from New York. Argentine questions arising in a US matter are handled by the Argentine entity, and we tell you when a question has crossed.
  • We are not a general US practice. Matters with no Argentine or Latin American dimension are not what we do, and larger New York firms will serve them better.
  • We are admitted in New York. Questions of another state's law are referred, not assumed. [ADMISSION SCOPE — VERIFY]
  • We do not engage with US government on behalf of foreign interests. That raises registration obligations and sits outside both the legal and advisory offerings. [FARA / LOBBYING SCOPE — VERIFY]
