Why so much of this sits in New York

Argentine businesses do not choose New York law because they prefer it. They choose it because it is the price of access to institutional capital — a body of contract law with deep precedent, courts that have decided the hard questions, and remedies an international lender can price with confidence.

The practical consequence for an Argentine client is that the most consequential document in their business is often one drafted in a legal tradition they do not operate in, in a language that is not their working language, containing representations about facts that sit in Buenos Aires. Understanding what those representations actually commit them to is not a translation problem. It is the whole problem.
