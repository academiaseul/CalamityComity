Publication gate

No record renders publicly without a verified admission field, or — for advisory records — an explicit non-legal marker. A record with an empty admission cannot be published by anyone, including an administrator. This enforces §35 in the data layer rather than in review.

Two decisions worth reviewing

Admission, not location, is the primary filter. Most firm directories lead with office and seniority. In a cross-border practice the first question a client actually has is which bar covers their question — and a lawyer sitting in Buenos Aires may be admitted in New York. Filtering by office would answer the wrong question.

Advisory professionals appear in the same directory, marked. They have no admission and their work is not privileged, and both facts are carried on the card rather than discovered on the profile. Keeping them in a separate directory would hide the distinction; keeping them unmarked would misrepresent it.

No photography. Per Phase 04 §11 — a directory of names, admissions and languages is what a client filters on, and headshot grids are the most interchangeable pages on the internet. This stays true when the roster is disclosed.
